## 2. For patients

If you booked an appointment or joined a video consultation through a clinic that uses OpenORDO:

- **Your clinic is responsible for your care and your records.** Contact the clinic with any medical questions or concerns.
- **Do not use OpenORDO to get medical advice** or to treat an emergency. Contact your doctor.
- **In a medical emergency, call your local emergency number immediately** (for example 112 in India, 911 in the United States) or go to the nearest emergency department. Do not wait for an online appointment or a call back.
- Booking through a public booking page does not guarantee an appointment slot, a particular outcome, or a doctor's availability. The clinic confirms and manages appointments.
- The information a clinic shows on its booking page (services, doctors, qualifications, hours) is provided and controlled by the clinic. OpenORDO does not verify it.

## 3. For clinicians and clinic staff

You are responsible for:

- **Clinical judgement.** All diagnoses, treatment plans, prescriptions and advice are yours. Use your professional judgement, not the software, to decide what is right for each patient.
- **Accuracy of records.** OpenORDO stores what you enter. It does not check that diagnoses, dosages, allergies, medication names or notes are complete, correct or clinically appropriate. Verify allergy information, dosing and interactions using reliable clinical sources before prescribing or treating.
- **Legal and professional compliance.** You must hold the registrations and licences needed for your practice, follow your medical council's code of conduct and the laws where you and your patient are located, including record-keeping, consent, prescribing and telemedicine rules.
- **Patient consent and identification.** Confirm identity and obtain consent before recording data, sharing links, or conducting a video visit.
- **Backup plans.** Do not rely solely on OpenORDO for information critical to safety. Keep a way to reach patients and access essential information if the service is unavailable.

## 4. Video consultations

- Video consultations may not be suitable for every patient or condition. The doctor decides if a remote consultation is appropriate, and whether the patient should be seen in person.
- Video quality depends on the patient's and doctor's devices and internet connection and on the video provider. Calls can drop, lag or fail. OpenORDO does not guarantee quality or availability.
- The screen shows whether the other participant is connected and whether their camera or microphone is on. This is an indicator only, and should not be relied on as the only way to confirm someone can see or hear you.
- **OpenORDO does not record consultations.** If a clinic records a call using other tools, the clinic is responsible for consent and storage.
- Video consultation is **not for emergencies**.

## 5. E-Prescriptions

The E-Prescriptions add-on helps a registered prescriber create structured, printable prescriptions. It does not:

- check clinical suitability, dosage safety, interactions, contraindications, allergies or duplicate therapies;
- verify the prescriber's registration, or the legal validity of a prescription in any jurisdiction, or that a pharmacy will accept it;
- replace a signature, digital signature or other authentication required by the law where you practise.

The prescriber must ensure each prescription complies with local law. Drug lists and autocomplete are convenience aids and may be incomplete or out of date.
